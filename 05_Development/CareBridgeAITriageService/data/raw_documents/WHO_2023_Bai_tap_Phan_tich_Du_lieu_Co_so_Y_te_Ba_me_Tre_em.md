About the materials

To accompany the World Health Organization (WHO) document Analysis and use of health facility
data: guidance for maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health programme managers, a set of
presentation and exercise materials have been developed for use as a framework to facilitate
workshops or other forums for strengthening capacity to analyse and use maternal, newborn, child
and adolescent health (MNCAH) data.
Not all content in the presentation sessions or exercise book might be applicable in all cases, thus
one can select relevant content from these materials for the specific setting and audience for which
they will be used. Similarly, the materials may be tailored to the context in which they are being
used by employing local, national, or regional data examples and information. The exercises follow
a logical flow of data analysis and use concepts that matches the series of presentation slides. They
can also be adapted as needed.

Objectives
The purpose of the presentation and exercise materials is to introduce MNCAH indicators for
routine reporting through routine health information systems (RHIS) as recommended in the WHO
document Analysis and use of health facility data: guidance for maternal, newborn, child and
adolescent health programme managers; review concepts and methods used to assess the quality
of and analyse RHIS data; and strengthen the capacity of MNCAH managers to interpret and use
MNCAH data for decision-making.

Audience
The intended audience for the guidance and capacity strengthening materials is MNCAH managers
at all levels of the health system. The materials may also be of interest to partner organizations
seeking to introduce and use Analysis and use of health facility data: guidance for maternal,
newborn, child and adolescent health programme managers.

Suggested flow for using the materials
The materials introduce and review different concepts related to collection, reporting, quality,
analysis, interpretation, and use of MNCAH data collected through RHIS. They are organized as
presentation sessions followed by exercises to reinforce comprehension of the content. In a
workshop setting, the exercises may be done through a mix of plenary discussion, group work, and
individual completion, after which the responses or key points should be reviewed.

Data quality

 The exercises in this section correspond to the presentation Data quality considerations for
 MNCAH managers.
 Data should accurately describe the progress and performance of a health programme or health
 sector. Before data analysis is conducted, it is important to do a thorough data quality assessment
 to identify, investigate, and correct any errors. If errors cannot be explained or corrected, it is
 strongly advised that the data are used only with appropriate caution and caveats.
 Some of the most common data quality problems associated with routine health facility data include
 the following.
 y Incomplete data resulting from incomplete reporting, poor reporting timeliness, and/or lack of
 reporting by the private sector.
 y Inconsistent data that do not accord with other observations, such as outliers, assessments of
 consistency over time or consistency among related indicators, and external comparisons.
 Overreporting caused by data entry errors should also be considered. There may also be
 discrepancies among the denominators used.
 Review the completeness of data
 Before analysing the year-to-year trend in MNCAH data, you should confirm that the completeness
 of reporting has not changed substantially over time. Completeness is the percentage of expected
 reports that have been submitted to a higher level (i.e. facility reports submitted to the district level,
 and district reports submitted to the national level).

 variations (e.g. seasonal variation, campaigns, etc.). When a data value for a specific facility or

 pregnancies in 2019, 2020, and 2021 are: 1 567 000, 1 619 000, and 1 672 000 respectively. The

 at 403 per 100 000 live births. An estimated 74% of women received ANC for their most recent birth
 and 43% had at least four ANC visits during their last pregnancy. Almost half of the births (48%) in
 2019 occurred in a health facility.
