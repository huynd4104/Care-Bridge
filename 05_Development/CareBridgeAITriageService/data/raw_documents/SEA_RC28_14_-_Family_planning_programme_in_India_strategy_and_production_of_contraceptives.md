### FAMILY PLANNING PROGRAMME I N INDIA STRATEGY AND PRODUCTION OF CONTRACEPTIVES

(Paper submitted by the Government of India)

The Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) has recommended the use of combination p i l l s . A t present, India is entirely dependent on imports f o r ensuring the availability of an adequate number of p i l l s for use i n the programme. It is absolutely necessary that a continuous and adequate supply of o r a l contraceptives be ensured and self-sufficiency achieved within the country. Based on the experience of the ICMR with the low-dose combination p i l l s , the following preparations have been recommended f o r introduction i n the national family planning programme:

( i ) Norethisterone acetate 1.0 mg Ethinyl estradiol 50 mcg

( i i ) Dl-noregestrel 0.5 mg Ethinyl estradiol 30 mcg

For the production of some of the components of o r a l contraceptives, diosgenin, derived from tubers of dioscorea, is necessary not only f o r the synthesis of contraceptive steroids but also for other drugs l i k e corticoids. Dioscorea is cultivated commercially i n limited quantities i n the Kashmir Valley and around Bangalore. Most of the dioscorea tubers now being produced i n Kashmir a r e being sold exclusively by the regional research laboratory i n Jammu and Kashmir, through a system of tenders, t o the highest bidder. Dioscorea tubers must be regarded as a scarce resource of national importance. A s such, there is a strong case f o r ensuring t h a t t h i s material is made available a t reasonable fixed prices t o all licensed manufacturers of steroids.

Since adequate cultivation is of national importance, a central agency w i l l have to be entrusted with the responsibility of growing adequate quantities of dioscorea under contract. Prices should be high enough t o stimulate interest, and a l l such crops should be purchased by the central agency.

Against guaranteed prices and purchase of the entire output, the agency could a l s o ensure finance t o farmers from banks. The ICKR and the Indian Council f o r Agricultural Research (ICAR) could jointly suggest t o the Government the establishment of such an agency.

The Indian Drugs and Pharmaceuticals Limited (IDPL), a public sector undertaking, should set up f a c i l i t i e s f o r the production of diosgenin from dioscorea. A s a starting point, IDPL is taking up the tabletting of oral contraceptives from imported raw material. In view of the higher seeding t o crop r a t i o (1:10), plant tissue culture is suitable for quick propagation, and the National Chemical Laboratory must consider giving priority t o the culture of dioscorea.

Alternate sources of steroids such a s stigmassterol from sugarcane and press mud of the soya bean should be explored. Given support and a commitment t o purchase o r a l contraceptives i n bulk, it may be possible to meet the requirements of the country i n due course.

Noregestrel, ethinyl estradiol and norethisterone a r e a t present being imported, and can be replaced by local manufacture over a period of two and a half years, by the use of known synthetic soup. The starting point should be diosgenin f o r ethinyl estradiol and norethisterone. Noregestrel could be made i n adequate quantities during t h i s time by t o t a l synthesis.

Virtually a l l the raw materials w i l l be of Indian origin within three years, and self-sufficiency and self-reliance can be attained. The technological development should involve the Central Drug Research Institute, Lucknow, Indian I n s t i t u t e of Science, Bangalore, and a few Indian companies i n both the public and the private sectois. A s noted above, diosgenin is produced under the aegis of the State Government of Jammu and Kashmir, and the Government could easily procure and earmark t h i s quantity of diosgenin f o r steroids synthesis, and control the supply of diosgenin ensuring that it went to bona f i d e manufacturers only. There a r e a few Indian firms i n the country which have the background necessary for the manufacture of steroids, and with some technical support, these firms could also be encouraged t o manufacture the quantities of t h i s steroid which a r e necessary f o r the programme. Similarly, there a r e a few research laboratories which have labor a t o r i e s f o r the manufacture of steroids on a laboratory scale.

The country is self-sufficient i n the production of ethinyl estradiol, the other ingredient necessary f o r the formulation of combination p i l l s .
