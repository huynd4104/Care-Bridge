## Who we are

The UN Special Programme in Human Reproduction (HRP) advances

evidence-based sexual and reproductive health and rights around the

world. For five decades, HRP has generated evidence that has anchored

health systems and improved the lives of millions of people, often

addressing topics considered too sensitive or controversial by many.

Sexual and reproductive health are fundamental to the right everyone

has to the highest attainable standard of physical and mental health

and well-being. Furthermore, access to comprehensive sexual and

reproductive health services is critical to progressing towards universal

health coverage and leaving no one behind. In line with the United

Nation’s Sustainable Development Goals, HRP takes a holistic view of

sexual and reproductive health, spanning the entire life course, with a

focus on women, girls, adolescents and marginalized populations.

## What we do

HRP is committed to scientific and ethical rigour, to gender equality and relevance of work to national priorities. Thanks to the work of HRP and its partners, millions of people around the world have improved access to sexual and reproductive health information, services and rights – essential for health, gender equality and wider economic and social development.

Our multi-disciplinary team of scientists and medical experts supports and coordinates work on a global scale, synthesizing research through systematic reviews of literature, conducting research, building research capacity in low-income countries and developing and disseminating tools. In a world of ever-increasing information, misinformation and disinformation, HRP plays a critical role in ensuring everyone has access to the latest and most accurate research, evidence and guidance.

The past five decades include groundbreaking research on safe and comprehensive abortion care, contraception, violence against women, self-care, sexual health and respectful and quality care during and after pregnancy and delivery, with special attention to adolescents and humanitarian settings. HRP, with our partners, is the evidence backbone for guidelines, protocols and policies related to all aspects of sexual and reproductive health.

WHO establishes the Expanded Programme of Research, Development and Research Training in Human Reproduction.

A ten-year, 13-centre HRP study, the largest of its kind, provided evidence of the safety of oral contraceptives regarding the risk of cancer, thus providing more choice of safe contraception options.

HRP published the first WHO laboratory manual for the examination and processing of human semen which is key in diagnosing some causes of infertility. In its sixth edition, this manual continues to be widely used around the world.

### Publication

HRP with WHO published the first “Medical Eligibility Criteria for Contraceptive Use” with the latest guidance on the safety of different contraceptive methods for women with specific health conditions and characteristics. It is now in its fifth edition.

HRP research shows magnesium sulfate (a cheap, commonly available drug) can halve the risks and possible death of high blood pressure in pregnant women.

HRP trials explored the safety and efficacy of misoprostol and mifepristone. These were added to the WHO complementary List of Essential Medicines as a medical abortion regimen and important reproductive health medication.

### More achievements here

### Study

HRP published the first international prevalence data on women’s health and domestic violence revealing widespread violence by male partners in ten countries. This report was groundbreaking, providing evidence that violence against women and girls can lead to a range of adverse physical, mental and psychosocial health outcomes, including negative impacts on sexual and reproductive health, in all regions.

### Study

HRP’s Kesho Bora Study (“Preventing mother-to-child transmission of HIV during breastfeeding”) showed that a combination of antiretroviral drugs during pregnancy and delivery is safe and efficacious. This led to WHO guidelines on antiretrovirals, prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV and infant feeding.

© UNICEF / Nesbitt

2019 Together with WHO, HRP revised its consolidated guideline on self-care interventions for health to support access to, uptake of, and use of self-care interventions, particularly for sexual and reproductive health and rights.

### Event

HRP demonstrated that the efficacy and safety of heat-stable carbetocin is comparable with oxytocin (the gold standard intervention) for the prevention of postpartum haemorrhage, the leading cause of maternal death. This offers another option to health professionals to save lives during childbirth.

## Together with our partners

Within a supportive environment of Member States and UN agencies, HRP brings together scientists and academic institutions, health care providers and their professional associations, government ministries and policymakers and civil society organizations. As a multi-stakeholder partnership mandated to work in all aspects of sexual and reproductive health and rights, we are a trusted global convener and knowledge broker of research and evidence for advancing health, rights and gender equality.

We work with our partners on a package of comprehensive sexual and reproductive health services. These include: antenatal care, labour and childbirth care, postnatal care, services for contraception and family planning, infertility, sexual health, prevention of violence, including female genital mutilation and intimate partner and sexual violence, comprehensive sexuality education, abortion and ectopic pregnancy. We also continue to build an evidence base on HIV, sexually transmitted infections and reproductive system cancers.

### HRP is organized across six units of work with a Secretariat coordinating governance, administration and communications

 Contraception and fertility care: works to ensure that people’s human

## Impact at the country level

High quality research and shared learning are critical to achieving health for all, but access and opportunity are not equitable. HRP’s policies ensure open access to our research so that it is always free and available to everyone.

Core to our principles, HRP also promotes research capacity strengthening around the world. The HRP Alliance, a dynamic network of institutions conducting research in sexual and reproductive health and rights, drives efforts to shift research power, agenda-setting and autonomy towards marginalized contexts. HRP enables individuals to emerge as next-generation scholars of sexual and reproductive health and rights, in turn empowering institutions to lead on responsive, effective health policies and programmes in every country.

HRP’s relationship with WHO is unique. HRP draws on WHO’s credibility and leverage to conduct research

and to set global standards on politically charged issues. WHO’s normative mandate enables HRP to impact

policy change at the global level and provides HRP the convening power to engage research institutions and

governments nationally and internationally. Hand-in-hand, HRP provides WHO and other cosponsors the

mechanism and expertise to investigate issues closely linked to reproduction and sexual health.

## The future of sexual and reproductive health

Sexual and reproductive health are integral to overall health and well-being; every one of us has needs that evolve from birth to death, every one of us has rights. Through an end-to-end approach based on science and evidence-driven decision making that does not shy away from controversy, HRP brings about real insight and change.

For over 50 years, HRP has been a disruptive, progressive force that has advanced justice and confronted systems of oppression through uncompromising, world-leading research and evidence-based advocacy.
