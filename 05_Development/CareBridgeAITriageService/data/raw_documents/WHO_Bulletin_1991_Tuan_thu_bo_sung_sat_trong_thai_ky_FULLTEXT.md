Discussion
The indicators used in the evaluation are relatively
easy to measure, make use of existing data, and have
immediate relevance to programme and clinical
management. Although they do not measure the
adequacy of diagnosis nor assess patient compliance
with the prescribed therapy, these indicators can be
useful for summarizing the complex situation related
to the following aspects of rational drug therapy:
adequacy of diagnostic and therapeutic knowledge;
qualitative indicators of general aspects of drug
therapy; and the specific drug treatment patterns for
certain defined diagnoses.
The results of the evaluation have been of direct
use for managing the rational drug therapy programme in Democratic Yemen, where it has led to
an extension of the distribution of drug kits and to a
training programme in the control area. A similar
approach is currently being followed in the Yemen
Arab Republic and the Sudan, and the method is
suitable for use in evaluating rational drug use in
other developing countries.

on health messages that reflect understanding of
