# WHO 2025 — Khung chuyển đổi chương trình sức khỏe bà mẹ, trẻ sơ sinh và thai chết lưu


## Nội dung trọng tâm đã Việt hóa


Khung WHO 2025 hỗ trợ quốc gia đánh giá vị trí hiện tại của chương trình sức khỏe bà mẹ, trẻ sơ sinh và phòng ngừa thai chết lưu, từ đó lựa chọn ưu tiên chiến lược cho giai đoạn chuyển tiếp tiếp theo. Tài liệu hướng tới việc sử dụng dữ liệu, xác định nút thắt, lựa chọn can thiệp có tác động và tổ chức thực thi theo bối cảnh hệ thống y tế.


Đây là công cụ hoạch định ở cấp chương trình/quốc gia, không phải bảng kiểm lâm sàng cho từng thai phụ. Khi đưa vào RAG, cần giữ rõ ranh giới giữa chỉ báo hệ thống, chiến lược triển khai và hướng dẫn chăm sóc cá nhân.


## Ranh giới sử dụng cho RAG


- Giữ nguyên năm xuất bản, quốc gia/khu vực, thiết kế nghiên cứu và đối tượng khi truy xuất số liệu.
- Không biến kết quả nghiên cứu, khảo sát hoặc tài liệu chiến lược thành chẩn đoán/phác đồ cho cá nhân.
- Khi có xung đột với hướng dẫn hiện hành, ưu tiên tài liệu hiện hành đã được thẩm định.
- Phụ lục nguyên ngữ bên dưới được giữ lại để hạn chế mất tri thức và hỗ trợ kiểm chứng.

Background and purpose
The purpose of the Maternal, newborn and stillbirth programmatic transition framework is to enable 
health planners to identify priority programmatic, health systems, community and multisector strategic 
choices to accelerate transition through phases of mortality transition to achieve the Sustainable 
Development Goals (SDGs).
This document is part of the package for the Maternal, newborn and stillbirth programmatic transition 
framework, which includes four files:
• background document;
• slides for sensitization;
• manual for making strategic choices;
• assessment tool (spreadsheet and digital version).
The process for developing the Maternal, newborn and stillbirth programmatic transition framework 
is included as Annex 1.
This package complements the Maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health programme review 
tool and the Microplanning tool (see Annex 2).
This document provides step-by-step instructions on how to use the Maternal, newborn and stillbirth 
programmatic transition framework to assess a country’s current status in the transition and to advance 
to the next transition phase by addressing programme and health systems barriers and social and 
multisector determinants of health. The tool could also be applied at the subnational level, depending 
on the purpose of the assessment. Although the sequence and frequency may vary according to the 
country context, the key steps to undertake the assessment and prioritization exercise are:
• sensitization, preliminary assessment and formation of a taskforce for transition assessment;
• technical assessment – data entry for the Transition assessment tool;
• technical workshop on transition assessment, mapping of problems and making strategic choices;
• high-level policy dialogue engaging relevant sectors;
• integrating findings into various development plans and tracking progress;
• completing the template for documenting implementation of the transition framework.2
Sensitization, preliminary assessment 
and formation of a taskforce for 
transition assessment
The transition assessment process is led by the national ministry of health. In countries with decentralized 
health systems, state or provincial governments can initiate the process. The programme manager(s) 
for maternal and newborn health will ideally be the point person(s) for undertaking the process. To 
initiate the process, the programme manager sensitizes the respective senior management, which may 
require preliminary quantitative data assessment for key maternal and newborn health indicators.
After sensitizing senior management in the ministry of health, a multisectoral taskforce is set up with 
key stakeholders critical to improving the status of maternal and newborn health. Key stakeholders 
might include key programme staff; key staff from health systems, planning and data departments; 
government authorities engaged in policy, planning and implementation within various sectors (e.g. 
governance, legal, infrastructure, nutrition, waste and sanitation); the ministry of women’s affairs; civil 
society champions; the ministry of finance; and United Nations agencies.
At this stage, meetings can be convened with individuals or groups to orient and engage them in the 
assessment process. The coordinating body or taskforce can continue to play a critical role in the 
transition assessment process, prioritizing strategies, and planning and integrating actions into various 
development plans, and can also be responsible for the monitoring progress over the years.
Countries develop the terms of reference and operational procedures of the taskforce as per the country 
context. A generic terms of reference for the Maternal, newborn and stillbirth programmatic transition 
framework taskforce is included in Annex 3.
The purpose of the meetings is to share and discuss the objectives and processes of the transition 
assessment process, and to discuss the scope and timeline for undertaking the process of assessment, 
planning and monitoring. The generic slides included in the Maternal, newborn and stillbirth 
programmatic transition framework package could be adapted to the country context for sharing in 
these sensitization meetings.
The first step for sensitization is to gather initial data to assign the country to a mortality phase of the 
transition model. The transition framework uses mortality cut-offs for maternal and newborn mortality 
and stillbirth to categorize a geographical unit into one of five phases (Table 1).Sensitization, preliminary assessment and formation of a taskforce for transition assessment

Several phases of transition can coexist in one country and can highlight inequities, such as between 
urban and rural areas, or between stable and displaced or marginalized populations. The transition 
process may not always be linear, and it is feasible to move from higher to lower mortality phases and 
from lower to higher mortality phases.
To move from one phase to the next, thresholds for all three mortality indicators (maternal mortality, 
newborn mortality and stillbirth rate) must be met. The mortality indicators may fall into different 
phases, but the overall categorization will be according to the lowest phase.
This preliminary assessment leverages mortality estimates and prepopulated country maternal and 
newborn health-related data. In subsequent steps, any other preferred data sources of mortality 
levels or maternal and newborn health indicators can be entered into the Transition assessment tool 
spreadsheet. A country may choose to use mortality data other than the United Nations mortality 
estimates. The first step is to gather these mortality data, enter them in Rows 16, 18 and 20 of the 
Transition assessment tool spreadsheet, and assign a phase of the transition framework. In future steps, 
the country assesses its performance across key maternal and newborn health-related indicators based 
on the phase it is placed in – which may depend on the preferred source of mortality data.
Expected output
• a high-level vision of a country’s performance across key maternal and newborn healthrelated indicators contextualized in the transition framework;
• terms of reference for the taskforce comprising of representatives from relevant sectors.4
Transition assessment: data entry using 
the transition assessment tool
Once the country is assigned a phase for the mortality indicators, the taskforce undertakes the detailed 
transition assessment and planning exercise. The Transition assessment tool includes five internally 
aligned sheets, numbered 1 to 5. The Transition assessment tool is designed to help assess the current 
phase of a country in transition, identify major structural bottlenecks, and make strategic decisions to 
move to the next phase of transition. The transition assessment does not require new data collection, 
but it is an opportunity to synthesize existing data.
Sheet 1: Transition assessment
Using Sheet 1 of the spreadsheet, the country can be assigned a phase for each of the indicators. The 
indicators are grouped into four broad categories – MNH [maternal and newborn health] programme 
(Part 1), health systems levers (Part 2), community enablers (Part 3) and multisector enablers (Part 4) 
(Fig.  1). The transition framework includes the three primary health-care components – health 
systems, and community and multisector policies and actions – and incorporates the strategic and 
operational policy levers of the Operational framework for primary health care (https://iris.who.int/
handle/10665/337641).

Once a phase is assigned to the country, a more in-depth analysis is needed to review various health 
and non-health components to identify and prioritize barriers and make strategic choices to accelerate 
progress to the next phase. It is necessary to appoint a focal point to collect and enter the data into 
the Transition assessment tool. Since the assessment includes data inputs within and outside the 
health sector, relevant non-health partners, members of the taskforce and individuals will need to 
be engaged and consulted. Exemplars in Global Health has developed an interactive tool that could 
be used as a resource for prepopulated mortality estimates and data across maternal and newborn 
health indicators and creating visuals. There are also other tools that can be used to complement this 
process (see Annex 1).
Each of the four broad areas (Parts 1 to 4) includes several quantitative and qualitative data elements 
that are important for moving to the next phase of transition. Each element may or may not be relevant 
for a country. Based on the relevance and data availability, the relevant indicators for data entry can 
be selected, and the rest “hidden”.
Identify the status by entering the relevant data into Column C for the country or subnational area, 
related to the mortality risk factors, programme coverage, health policies and plans, health systems 
levers, community enablers and multisector enablers.
The reference figures and transition narratives in Columns  D to H have been pulled together, as 
benchmarks for each phase, from evidence-based guidelines, global health databases and the literature 
and indicate common characteristics for that element within that phase of the transition.
The data source, definition and how the quantitative benchmark was calculated for the respective 
indicator are provided in Columns I to K. These reference figures or transition narratives can be used 
to define the country status in Column C.
Highlight or underline the number or transition narrative in Columns D to H that corresponds to what 
you entered in Column C. This information informs the phase of transition for each indicator. Note 
that it is possible to be in different phases of transition for different indicators. Some of the indicators 
(e.g. preterm birth rate, low birth weight rate) include median or average values for each phase. The 
taskforce members may select the phase corresponding to the value closest to the country data. This 
is a critical exercise that will facilitate discussion on where to prioritize strategic choices to move to the 
next phase of transition. The data for the quantitative indicators can be found from reports, surveys 
and data systems, but country-level consultation will be required for the qualitative indicators. Some 
of the indicators listed do not include any reference or benchmark characterization for different phases, 
because plausible evidence was not available to characterize each phase for that indicator. In some 
cases, the indicators have been included after consultation with expert groups. A glossary of the key 
definitions used in the Transition assessment tool is included as an annex in the spreadsheet.
After completing each section, you will find several assessment questions to review based on the data 
entered. Enter your responses as relevant. Indicators, responses to questions and summaries of findings 
can be sorted or filtered by colour in Column A. Enter information into the summaries to ensure this 
information is automatically reflected in Column B (Findings) in Sheet 5: Summary and planning.6 Maternal, newborn and stillbirth programmatic transition framework: background document
Sheet 1: Transition Assessment contains the following sections:
• Part 1 – maternal and newborn health programme: includes indicators related to mortality, mortality 
risks, maternal and newborn health programme coverage including equity, and policies and plans.
• Part 2 – health systems and policy levers: includes health systems strategic and operational levers, 
such as policies and governance, workforce, financing, quality, models of care, commodities, 
monitoring and evaluation, infrastructure and the private sector.
• Part 3 – community enablers: includes community participation and engagement, community 
health workers, health literacy and practices influencing outcomes during pregnancy, birth and 
newborn periods.
• Part 4 – multisector enablers: includes the country’s non-health political, social and economic 
determinants that directly or indirectly shape health outcomes for women and newborns.
Expected output
• completed Sheet 1: Transition Assessment, including data and summaries on barriers and 
facilitators for advancing to the next phase.7
Technical workshop on transition 
assessment, mapping of problems and 
making strategic choices
The ministry of health convenes a technical workshop with the taskforce and other relevant technical 
staff to review the findings from the transition assessment and discuss the findings to make strategic 
choices to move to the next phase of transition. This technical workshop should include representatives 
from all relevant sectors.
Sheet 2: Problem map
The group reviews and discusses the findings from the transition assessment for each area. The group 
identifies the critical barriers and ranks major barriers or factors that have the highest impact on 
moving to the next phase. Using the fish diagram for problem mapping (Fig. 2), the group enters the 
most important problems (up to a maximum of three) for each category. A list of guiding questions is 
included on the right of the sheet.

Sheet 3: Menu of strategic choices
Sheet 3 facilitates the identification of problems, barriers and strategic choices to move to the next 
phase of transition (Fig. 3). Enter data, as agreed by the group, into Column C. Columns D to H provide 
probable choices for various settings. Note that the examples may or may not be relevant to your 
country’s context and needs. These strategic choices are drawn from the evidence base on key drivers, 
using a primary health-care approach. The list can be used to benchmark the desired outcome for each 
component. For example, if Country A is in Phase 1 for workforce density, given the present context, 
what choices will it prioritize to achieve the desired benchmarks to advance to the next phase?
The determinants of health perspective on mortality transition assumes there is no single intervention 
that will shift the country from one phase to the next. Instead, it assumes that multiple strategic choices 
need to be made to drive mortality transition. Enter summary choices as indicated after each section. 
The choices show automatically in Sheet 5: Summary and planning. Prioritize your choices based on 
the following question – what strategic policies and actions will have the most impact on driving the

Annex 1. Development process for 
the Maternal, newborn and stillbirth 
programmatic transition framework
Background
The Maternal, newborn and stillbirth programmatic transition framework can be used as a health 
systems and multisectoral tool to deliver on a specific programme area – maternal and newborn 
mortality and stillbirth – using a primary health-care approach. The transition framework was 
developed using an extensive process to review and synthesize evidence-based approaches informed 
by an expert group under the guidance of the Strategic and Technical Advisory Group of Experts for 
Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescent Health and Nutrition between 2022 and 2024. Feedback 
from partners and regional and country colleagues and pilot testing were conducted between 2023 
and 2024. The process of development and testing is described below.
Part 1: Developing the concept
Between 2020 and 2022, the World Health Organization (WHO) department of Maternal, Newborn, Child 
and Adolescent Health and Ageing (MCA) worked with the Exemplars in Maternal and Newborn Health 
project to support a series of case studies aimed at examining the success factors for reducing maternal 
and newborn mortality in a variety of countries. These analyses and published case studies formed the 
foundation of the integrated Maternal, newborn and stillbirth programmatic transition framework (1). 
The transition framework included characteristics of various transition phases. The combined model 
for a maternal and newborn mortality and stillbirth transition described the shifts in causes of death, 
intervention coverage, fertility, health systems characteristics, and service delivery strategies across 
the five phases of mortality transition, from higher to lower levels of mortality.
WHO MCA was interested in building on the concept of the transition framework to develop a practical 
programmatic tool to support countries or subnational geographical areas to accelerate progress 
in reducing mortality to achieve the Every Woman Every Newborn Everywhere (EWENE) targets and 
milestones. The transition framework concept provides a systematic process to understand the 
key characteristics of progress to inform strategic planning and identification of evidence-based 
approaches to reduce mortality and move to the next phase of transition based on benchmarking 
progress across phases.
The technical work was guided by an expert group under the guidance of the Strategic and Technical 
Advisory Group of Experts for Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescent Health and Nutrition. In 
addition, relevant departments and subject experts within the United Nations Population Fund, the 16 Maternal, newborn and stillbirth programmatic transition framework: background document
United Nations Children’s Fund and WHO were consulted, and feedback was gathered from regional 
and country colleagues through a series of regional EWENE technical consultations in 2025.
Consultations
• Strategic and Technical Advisory Group of Experts for Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescent Health 
and Nutrition (STAGE) working group, May 2023, November 2023, May 2024 and November 2024;
• Network for Improving Quality of Care for Maternal, Newborn and Child Health meeting, Accra, 
Ghana, March 2023;
• EWENE regional workshops – Bangkok, Thailand, March 2024, Lusaka, Zambia, July 2024, and 
Dakar, November 2024.
Part 2: Developing the tool
The analyses of the mortality cut-offs and other critical components for each phase were based on an 
in-depth analysis published in 2023. This analysis formed the basis for the expanded tool. A team of 
experts adapted the transition framework based on four categories in line with the Primary Health-care 
Framework – maternal and newborn health programmatic elements, health systems determinants, 
community enablers, and multisectoral determinants (2). Within each of these components, the 
critical elements were mapped, looking at policy and implementation levels. This mapping was done 
in collaboration with experts in each of the areas and by reviewing the literature, relevant technical 
documents and case studies. Benchmarks for each phase of transition for each element were developed 
based on review of the literature, relevant technical documents and case studies. All documents are 
referenced in the spreadsheet tool (3, 4).
The menu of strategic choices for each element across the four components was developed for each 
of the phases of transition. The strategies reflect various enabling efforts and interventions adopted 
by countries at various phases of the mortality transition. They were developed based on an extensive 
review of the literature, including global guidelines, recommendations and standards. For example, 
the UHC Compendium (5) was used to inform prioritized maternal and newborn health interventions 
across the stages of transition. Strategies for health systems strengthening indicators and strategic 
choices were sourced from expert consultations, the Primary Health Care Performance Initiative 
(PHCPI) Progression Model (6), the WHO General 14th General Programme of Work health systems 
strengthening set of indicators, and global health systems guidance on health financing (7). Elements of 
the community engagement and multisector strategic choices were sourced from the WHO Handbook 
on Social Participation for Universal Health Coverage (8), the PHCPI Progression Model (6), and exemplar 
case studies on reduction in maternal and newborn mortality.
The determinants of transition are complex and multisectoral, and there can be high levels of uncertainty 
in relation to specifying strategic choices for each phase of transition at the global level. To manage this 
limitation, the guidance and tool identify strategic choices as being indicative of a general pathway for 
decision-making towards universal health coverage, rather than a set of specific planned steps. This 
enables countries to adapt the menu of choices to their country conditions.Annex 1. Development process for the Maternal, newborn and stillbirth programmatic transition framework

Step 3: Pilot testing
The transition framework was piloted in Indonesia, Liberia, Malawi and Niger in collaboration with 
regional and country partners. The aim was to test the feasibility of using the transition framework for 
the assessment and prioritizing of strategic choices to refine national plans. Key feedback included 
the following:
• Looking at data in a different way by using the Primary health-care framework provides an 
opportunity to shift the way of thinking to address maternal and newborn health challenges.
• Depending on the availability of data the tool can be used focusing at national and subnational 
levels, such as, in high-burden districts.
• Technical support and cooperation are needed to assist with the process, data analysis, guidance 
on strategic choices and overall scale-up.
• A multisector and health systems approach is required for maternal and newborn health, with 
opportunities to integrate with health and development planning.
• Monitoring and evaluation of the approach will require systematic documentation of lessons 
learned from implementation.

Annex 3. Maternal, newborn and

framework: generic terms of reference

Background
The programmatic transition assessment process for maternal and newborn health and prevention of 
stillbirths requires a combined effort of stakeholders from multiple sectors in the country to engage in 
data inputs, analysis, prioritizing problems and making strategic choices for implementation through 
a number of development programme plans that are not necessarily limited to the health sector.
Key staff from health programmes and systems, planning and data departments, government authorities 
engaged in policy, planning and implementation within various relevant sectors (e.g. governance, legal, 
infrastructure, nutrition, waste and sanitation), the ministry of women’s affairs, civil society champions, 
the ministry of finance and United Nations agencies are required to be engaged from the beginning of 
the process. The ministry of health focal person should contact the individuals and groups to orient 
and engage them in the assessment and prioritization process.
Purpose
The taskforce plays a critical role in the transition assessment process, prioritizing strategies, planning 
and integrating actions into various development plans. The taskforce is also responsible for the 
monitoring progress over the years.
Responsibilities
The key roles and responsibilities of the taskforce include the following:
• developing or adapt the terms of reference;
• conducting the transition assessment;
• convening and participating at the technical workshop to finalize the assessment findings and 
develop strategic choices;
• engaging with higher-level authorities to share and advance the assessment process;
• ensuring incorporation of prioritized strategies and actions into development plans;
• monitoring implementation and identifying bottlenecks;
• convening regular follow-up meetings to track progress.
Members
Members of the taskforce are determined by the country context and needs.22
